## 1. Purpose

A personal-use Monte Carlo simulator that lets the user honestly compare investing the same capital in **leveraged Australian residential investment property** versus **AU/global shares** over a multi-decade horizon, under realistic AU tax rules and with explicit handling of the fairness traps that make most existing calculators misleading.

User context: Adelaide SA. Real ongoing allocation decision. Hobby + real money. No public ship — no SEO, no AFSL disclaimers, no monetisation, no account systems.

The tool's signature features (none of which existing AU calculators offer together):

- **Probability framing** — headline output is "P(property beats shares)" across 5,000 Monte Carlo trials, not a single fake terminal-wealth number.
- **Two normalisation modes** — Realistic (what investors actually do) and Fair fight (matched leverage exposure). Mode C "Asset only" was cut as academic.
- **Equal outside-cash contributions** — both strategies deploy identical total capital each year. No silent subsidy.
- **Solvency tracking** — flags trials where property cashflow exceeds the user's serviceability ceiling, surfaces "P(strategy stays solvent)" alongside "P(strategy wins)."
- **Year-by-year cashflow stress** — visualises the year-7 squeeze when rates rise.
- **Standard / Advanced inputs split** — typical investors see a clean default form; power users get σ overrides, correlation knob, and Mode B counterfactual toggle behind a single expander.

### Module responsibilities

- **`config.py`** — all magic numbers in one place. Stage 3 brackets, μ/σ defaults, portfolio profile presets, asset-type land-value proxies, `TRIALS = 5000`, `BAD_YEAR_PROBABILITY = 0.05`, `BAD_YEAR_COST = 15000`. When tax law changes, mostly edit this file.
- **`model/tax.py`** — pure functions. `marginal_tax`, `franking_credit_refund`, `cgt_payable(gain, holding_period_years, mtr, capital_works_claimed)`, `sa_stamp_duty`, `sa_land_tax`, `depreciation_for_year(property_age, year, building_cost)`. No I/O, no globals.
- **`model/inflation.py`** — `inflate(value, base_year, target_year, cpi=0.025)` and a vectorised version for arrays. Used for rent, costs, sale price (in nominal modelling) and for the display-deflator toggle.
- **`model/property_strategy.py`** — given one Monte Carlo trial's draws, produces the full 30-year cashflow series, applies inflation to rent and costs, applies the bad-year Bernoulli, computes the terminal sale event with cost-base adjustment for cumulative Div 43 claimed.
- **`model/shares_strategy.py`** — same, for shares. Applies annual dividend tax (regardless of DRP), applies the equal-outside-cash-contribution rule by accepting external contribution amounts per year from the property strategy.
- **`model/monte_carlo.py`** — vectorised 5,000-trial runner. Wrapped in `@st.cache_data` keyed on input hash so identical scenarios are not re-run.
- **`model/normalisation.py`** — applies Mode A (Realistic) or Mode B (Fair fight); Mode B has an `isolate_asset_quality: bool` parameter that pins shares loan rate to mortgage rate when True. Mode C cut in v2.1.
- **`model/solvency.py`** — checks each trial's annual property cashflow against the user's serviceability ceiling; flags first breach as a "forced sale" event and adjusts that trial's terminal wealth accordingly.
- **`app.py`** — Streamlit UI only. No business logic.
- **`tests/`** — pytest with worked examples cross-checked against ATO online tax calculator and RevenueSA stamp-duty calculator. **Test expected outputs must come from external authoritative sources, not from running the same model under test.**

## 5. Tax engine

All in `model/tax.py` as pure functions. Test fixtures cross-checked against ATO online calculator and RevenueSA online calculator.

### 5.1 FY2026 (2025–26) resident MTR brackets

| Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16% |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 30% |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37% |
| $190,001+ | 45% |

(Excludes Medicare Levy — out of scope per §2.)

### 5.2 Negative gearing

Net rental loss (rent − all deductible costs − loan interest − depreciation) deducted against the user's other income at MTR. Current rules: full deductibility, no quarantining.

UI displays a "law pending" warning until 13 May 2026 (Budget night + 1).

### 5.3 Franking credits

Standard individual treatment: gross-up dividends at applicable franking rate, apply MTR, allow refund of excess imputation credits in cash.

### 5.4 CGT 50% discount

Applied to capital gain on assets held > 12 months, then taxed at MTR. Both property and shares qualify for any reasonable horizon.

### 5.5 Depreciation — per property age

| Property age | Div 40 (plant & equipment) | Div 43 (capital works) | Cost-base clawback at sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| New build | Allowed: depreciate over effective life | Allowed: 2.5% of construction cost / yr | Cumulative Div 43 reduces cost base |
| Established post-May-2017 | **Blocked** (legislative change) | Allowed: 2.5% of original construction cost / yr (if known) | Cumulative Div 43 reduces cost base |
| Established pre-May-2017 | Allowed (grandfathered) | Allowed | Cumulative Div 43 reduces cost base |

Building cost defaults to **40% of purchase price for houses, 85% for apartments, 65% for townhouses** — the inverse of the land-value proxy in §15.1 (land + building = purchase price). User may override.

If user overrides annual depreciation deduction directly, the override is treated as Div 43-equivalent for cost-base clawback purposes (conservative).

### 5.6 SA stamp duty (FY2026)

RevenueSA bands. Implemented as a function `sa_stamp_duty(price)` returning duty + transfer fees. Approximate value at $700k: ~$32,330. Test fixtures: cross-check at $400k, $700k, $1.2m against RevenueSA online calculator.

### 5.7 SA land tax (FY2026)

RevenueSA bands. General threshold $833k as of 2025–26. Computed on unimproved land value, derived from the asset-type land-value proxy unless user overrides.

### 5.8 Property holding costs

Fully deductible: management, rates, insurance, maintenance, loan interest, depreciation (per §5.5), land tax. **All non-interest costs inflate annually by CPI** (§4.4 default 2.5%).

### 5.9 Selling costs treatment

Selling costs (agent commission ~2.0%, legal ~0.5%) **increase the cost base, reducing the capital gain**. Not deductible against rental income.

### 5.10 Budget-2026 handling

v1 ships current FY2026 rules only — no toggle. v1.1 (week of 12 May, after Budget night) adds:

- `tax_regime: Literal["fy2026_current", "fy2026_post_budget"]` parameter to relevant tax functions
- A radio button in the UI sidebar
- Parallel tests for the new regime
- Existing tests must continue to pass unchanged for the current regime

## 6. Comparison engine — three normalisation modes

User picks which question they're asking; tool runs the appropriate normalisation. UI: single radio at top of results panel; re-runs Monte Carlo on switch.

### Mode A — Realistic (default)

- Property: user-specified deposit, leveraged via mortgage to user-specified purchase price, **plus stamp duty and buying costs paid upfront from the user's pocket**.
- Shares: starts with the **same total upfront cash as property** = deposit + stamp duty + buying costs. No leverage.
- This is what 99% of investors actually do. Property looks better mostly because of leverage, not asset quality. Honest about that.

### Mode B — Fair fight (matched total exposure)

- Property: same as Mode A.
- Shares: starts with the same total upfront cash, but additionally takes a margin loan to match property's total exposure ($700k in the default scenario). Margin loan rate per §4.3.
- **Margin-call warning banner** (always visible when Mode B is active): *"Mode B does not model margin-call risk. In a 30%+ share crash, your margin lender may force a sale at the bottom — this risk is real and is not captured in the distribution above."*
- **`isolate_asset_quality` toggle (Advanced panel only):** when On, shares margin loan rate is pinned to the mortgage rate. Default Off. Label clearly: *"Counterfactual: assumes you can borrow against shares on mortgage terms, which retail investors cannot in reality."*

### Mode C — REMOVED in v2.1 simplification pass

Mode C ("Asset only" — matched equity, zero leverage on both strategies) has been cut from v1. It answered an academic question ("is residential property a better unleveraged asset than a diversified share portfolio?") that requires a hypothetical the user couldn't execute (full purchase price in cash). Mode A (Realistic) and Mode B (Fair fight) cover the practical decision space.

If demand emerges, Mode C is a low-effort v1.1 addition since the comparison engine is already general.

## 7. Equal outside-cash contributions (renamed from "symmetric reinvestment")

Internal name in code: `equal_outside_cash_contributions`. UI label remains plain-English: *"Both strategies use the same money each year."*

In every mode, in every year, both strategies deploy identical total after-tax capital.

**Implementation:**

- Compute property strategy's net after-tax cashflow for the year.
- If negative (property strategy needs $X to maintain itself), the user funds it from outside income. The shares strategy contributes the same $X to its share portfolio in that year.
- If positive (property strategy has $Y of surplus), $Y is invested into shares **within the property strategy** (the property strategy ends with house + a small share portfolio of accumulated surplus).
- This surplus share portfolio is itself taxed annually on dividends and at terminal CGT on sale, like any other share holding.

**UI labelling:** the surplus portfolio inside the property strategy is shown as **"Overflow Wealth Invested"** on the wealth chart so the user understands why the property strategy ends up holding shares.

**Banner above the headline (always visible):** *"Both strategies deploy the same total capital each year. When property needs $X to feed negative gearing, shares invests the same $X. (Equal outside-cash contributions — ON.)"*

## 8. Monte Carlo engine

Implemented in `model/monte_carlo.py`, vectorised in numpy, wrapped in `@st.cache_data` keyed on input hash.

### 8.1 Random variables (per trial, per year)

For every variable, the value the user enters in §4 is the mean (μ). A new value is drawn each year using the σ shown here. User can override σ in the Advanced panel.

| Variable | Default μ | Default σ | Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property capital growth | 5.5% | 11.0% | Normal |
| Share total return | 8.5% (Blended) | 15.0% | Normal |
| Rental yield (relative to value) | (= user input) | 0.5pp | Normal |
| Vacancy weeks | (= user input) | 1 week | Normal, floored at 0 |
| Loan rate | (= user input) | 1.0pp | Normal |

(Bernoulli "bad-year event" was cut in the v2.1 simplification pass; see §0 changelog. Lumpy real-life risks are now covered loosely by the widened σ on yield and vacancy, with serviceability tracking as the safety net.)

### 8.2 Correlation

Property capital growth and share total return correlated (default 0.3) via Cholesky decomposition. The slider with quick-picks at -0.1 / 0.3 / 0.6 lives in the Advanced panel; the default of 0.3 is applied silently for users who never open Advanced. Other variables independent.

(§8.3 "Stress-test scenario presets" was cut in the v2.1 simplification pass; see §0 changelog. The Monte Carlo with widened loan-rate σ already shows rate variability.)

### 8.4 Trial count

`TRIALS = 5000` in `config.py`. Hidden from user. Yields ±~0.5% standard error on headline metric.

### 8.5 Outputs

Per trial: terminal after-tax wealth (both strategies), year-by-year cashflow series (both), year-by-year wealth-on-paper series (both), boolean "forced sale by serviceability breach" flag for property strategy.

Aggregated: P(property > shares); P(property strategy stays solvent); 10/50/90 percentile terminal wealth per strategy; median year-by-year cashflow per strategy; worst-decile cashflow path; total outside cash contributed (median).

## 9. Solvency tracking (new)

Implemented in `model/solvency.py`.

### Logic

- User specifies `max_annual_top_up` in §4.1 (default $20,000).
- For each Monte Carlo trial, in each year, compute property strategy's required out-of-pocket contribution (negative cashflow).
- If the contribution exceeds `max_annual_top_up` in any year, mark that trial as "forced sale at year *t*."
- For "forced sale" trials, terminal property wealth is calculated as if the property were sold in year *t* at that year's notional price (with applicable CGT, selling costs, loan payout). The trial then stops accumulating property exposure but continues accumulating the shares strategy normally.

### UI surfacing

- "P(property strategy stays solvent) = X%" appears as one of the four supporting metrics under the headline.
- A separate chart shows the distribution of "year of forced sale" across trials that fail.
- An inline note: *"In Y% of simulated futures, you would have run out of cash before year T and been forced to sell. The 'P(property beats shares)' headline includes these futures."*

This is the most important defensive feature in the model. A 53% win rate is meaningless if 30% of those wins required cash injections the user can't fund.

## 10. Inflation handling (now explicit)

`model/inflation.py` provides `inflate(value, base_year, target_year, cpi=0.025)`.

Applied to:

- **Rent** — gross rent inflates at CPI annually within each Monte Carlo trial.
- **Holding costs** — rates, insurance, maintenance, management fees as % of rent (so management auto-tracks rent inflation).
- **Loan interest** — *not* inflated; computed from outstanding loan balance × current loan rate (which itself drifts stochastically).
- **Property purchase price growth** — handled by the capital-growth random variable, not via separate CPI.
- **Sale price** — implicit via cumulative capital growth.
- **Display deflator** — when "today's dollars" toggle is On, all output dollar figures are deflated by `(1 + cpi) ** years_from_now`.

Without this, a 25-year model is broken — static $28k rent against static $8k maintenance over 30 years systematically misrepresents both strategies.

## 11. Stress events — REMOVED in v2.1 simplification pass

The bad-year Bernoulli event (5% probability, $15k impact) was cut as part of the simplification pass. Lumpy real-life property risks (tenant default, major capex, repairs) are now captured loosely by:

- Widened σ on rental yield (0.5pp) — accommodates yield collapses from tenant turnover
- Widened σ on vacancy (1 week) — accommodates extended vacancy events
- Solvency tracking (§9) — flags any year where cashflow exceeds the user's serviceability ceiling, regardless of cause

If demand emerges, distinct stochastic events with separate probabilities are a low-effort v1.1 addition.

## 12. Portfolio profile presets (new)

`model/__init__` exposes a `PORTFOLIO_PROFILES: dict` with three named entries (§4.3 table). UI: dropdown selector that, on change, sets all four coupled values (μ, σ, dividend yield, franked %). User can then override any single value individually.

Rationale: franking and return distribution are intrinsically coupled (an ASX-only portfolio has high franking; a global portfolio has low franking). Letting the user set them independently invites incoherent inputs.

The supporting-metrics row is the second-most important addition in v2. The user sees four numbers that together answer the *real* personal-decision question: "do I win, on average; in the worst case, can I afford it; and does the strategy actually survive?"

## 14. Testing

`pytest` in `tests/`. Coverage targets:

### Critical: external validation of expected outputs

Test fixtures' expected values come from external authoritative sources, not from running the model:

- **Stamp duty:** $400k / $700k / $1.2m investment property purchase → expected duty cross-checked against [RevenueSA online calculator](https://www.revenuesa.sa.gov.au/stamp-duty/rate-of-stamp-duty).
- **Income tax:** test cases at $50k / $90k / $150k / $250k taxable income → expected tax cross-checked against [ATO simple tax calculator](https://www.ato.gov.au/calculators-and-tools/tax-withheld-calculator).
- **CGT:** $200k gain held 5 years at MTR 37% → expected CGT cross-checked manually (gain × 50% × 37%).
- **Franking refund:** $700 fully franked dividend at MTR 19% → expected refund cross-checked manually.
- **Land tax:** $700k house in SA at 2025–26 thresholds → cross-checked against RevenueSA land tax calculator.

These fixtures must be **manually entered with the source URL and date in a comment**, not generated by Claude.

### Other tests

- `model/property_strategy.py` and `model/shares_strategy.py`: at least 3 known-good full-year cashflow tests each, hand-calculated.
- `model/normalisation.py`: tests proving each mode produces correct equity / leverage allocation, including the Mode B isolate-asset-quality toggle.
- `model/solvency.py`: tests that a trial breaching the ceiling is correctly flagged and terminated.
- `model/inflation.py`: round-trip test (inflate then deflate = identity); spot-check at known horizons.
- `model/monte_carlo.py`: determinism (fixed seed → reproducible); convergence (5,000 trials → ±1% stable percentiles); cache correctness (`@st.cache_data` returns identical output for identical inputs).

## 15. Open assumptions (deliberate v1 simplifications)

1. **Land tax proxy** — house 60%, apartment 15%, townhouse 35% of purchase price as unimproved value. SA-specific override exposed.
2. **Single-property assumption** — portfolio of properties out of scope.
3. **No margin call modelling in Mode B** — replaced by warning banner. v1.1 may model.
4. **Loan rate variability shared between IO and P&I phases** — same μ/σ throughout loan life.
5. **Depreciation as constant per year for established properties** — Div 43 is constant by definition; Div 40 (only for new builds) declines with effective-life schedule but v1 uses a flat estimate. Acceptable.
6. **No transaction costs on rebalancing within share portfolio** — DRP free; periodic rebalancing not modelled.
7. **Buy-and-hold share portfolio** — no mid-period sales, so no CGT events except at terminal sale. Small bias in favour of shares.
8. **Lumpy real-life property risks are captured loosely by widened σ on yield + vacancy, not via discrete stochastic events.** Bad-year Bernoulli was cut in v2.1 simplification. Solvency tracking is the safety net for catastrophic cashflow squeezes. v1.1 may add distinct stochastic events.
9. **Portfolio profile presets are eyeball estimates** — not derived from a fitted model. Reasonable for a personal tool; documented in `config.py` with rationale.
10. **Rate scenarios (RBA hike paths) are not modelled deterministically in v1** — captured via stochastic loan-rate σ = 1.0pp. v1.1 may add explicit overlay scenarios.

## 18. Decisions made on the three open questions from review

For traceability, the calls I made on the three open questions left after the Codex/Gemini review:

1. **Push-backs:** Mode B keeps different rates as default with an opt-in "isolate asset quality" toggle. UI keeps P(property wins) as visual centrepiece + 4 supporting metrics underneath. Reasoning: Gemini's same-rate-in-Mode-B fix models a financing market that doesn't exist; Codex's 5-metric dashboard dilutes the differentiator. Hybrid wins both arguments.
2. **Portfolio profile preset for v1: in.** Franking and return distribution are intrinsically coupled; independent sliders invite incoherent inputs. One preset selector with three options is cheaper than three independent inputs the user might combine wrong.
3. **Serviceability failure: simple version.** Single input ("max annual top-up I can fund: $X"); any year exceeds = forced sale. Cumulative cash buffer goes in v1.1 backlog. Adds one number to the form, prevents the "winning trial that's actually unaffordable" trap.
