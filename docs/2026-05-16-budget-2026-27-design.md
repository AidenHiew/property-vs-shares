# Federal Budget 2026-27 — Restricted NG + Indexed CGT Regime
## Design proposal for review

**Date:** 2026-05-16
**Status:** Proposal — pending external review before implementation

---

## What this project is

A personal-use Monte Carlo simulator comparing AU residential investment property
vs ETF shares over a 5-40 year horizon. Single user — Adelaide-based registered
tax agent modelling his own real-money allocation decision. Not a commercial
product; not used by clients. Streamlit + Python; ~700 lines including tests.

**Status of v1:** Shipped 2026-05-11. 66/66 tests passing. Default scenario (700k
established post-2017 house, 20% deposit, 25y horizon, 37% MTR, SA): P(property
wins) ≈ 76%, median property terminal wealth $2.11M vs shares $1.14M, worst-year
out-of-pocket cash $15.9k, P(stays solvent) 99.7%.

---

## What the Budget announced (12 May 2026, 7:30pm AEST cutoff)

For established residential property *acquired after the cutoff*, effective
**1 July 2027** (FY2028+):

1. **Negative gearing restricted.** Rental losses can no longer offset salary,
   dividends, or other non-property income. Losses are **quarantined** to future
   rental income or capital gains on residential property, with carry-forward.
2. **CGT redesigned.** 50% discount → cost-base **CPI indexation** + **30%
   minimum effective tax rate** on the indexed gain.

**Carve-outs (full retention of current rules):**
- Properties acquired *before* the cutoff (full grandfathering, no expiry)
- New builds (any acquisition date)
- Build-to-rent, super, widely-held trusts

**Status:** announcement → expected to pass parliament → effective 1 Jul 2027.
**Not yet legislated.** User accepts modelling on the announced shape since both
changes are bundled and refusing to model the CGT side gives a misleading picture
of the regime as a whole.

## What we propose to model

A single sidebar selector **"Negative gearing & CGT regime"**:

| Option | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Current rules** | Status quo: full NG, 50% CGT discount. Default for new build / grandfathered. |
| **Budget 2026-27** | NG quarantined from FY2028; CGT uses indexed cost base + max(MTR, 30%). Default if user is buying an established property today. |

### NG quarantining mechanics

- Year 1 of the model (FY2027) keeps current rules — restriction kicks in from
  year 2 (FY2028) for a today-purchase. Configurable via
  `restricted_ng_start_year_index` (default = 1).
- Maintain a `quarantined_loss_pool`. Each restricted year:
  - **Rental loss:** `tax_on_property = 0`, add absolute loss to pool. No refund
    against salary.
  - **Rental surplus:** apply pool first (reducing taxable income to zero before
    paying tax), then tax the remainder at MTR. Pool drained accordingly.
- At sale, any remaining pool offsets the capital gain *before* CGT is computed.

### CGT indexation mechanics

When regime is `restricted_2027`:
- `indexed_purchase_price = purchase_price × (1 + cpi) ** horizon_years`
- `indexed_cost_base = indexed_purchase_price + selling_costs - cumulative_div43`
  (selling costs and prior Div 43 deductions stay at face value; only the original
  cost is indexed — mirrors the pre-1999 indexation system)
- `indexed_gain = sale_price - indexed_cost_base`, then apply quarantined-loss
  offset, then `cgt = max(0, indexed_gain) × max(MTR, 0.30)`

For a 25-year hold of a $700k property at 5.5% growth, 2.5% CPI, 37% MTR, no
losses: indexation lifts cost base by ~$551k; effective rate stays at MTR (37% >
30%). CGT roughly $540k vs ~$355k under current 50% discount → **~$185k more tax**
at sale. P(property wins) is expected to drop materially in the default scenario.

## Judgement calls (please push back if any of these are wrong)

1. **Single toggle for both NG and CGT.** The Budget bundles them under one
   cutoff and one effective date with the same carve-outs. Splitting them would
   let users enable mathematically impossible combinations (e.g. new-build CGT
   + restricted NG). Modelling them together is intentionally less flexible.
2. **Indexation only of original purchase price.** Selling costs and Div 43
   deductions are kept nominal. Reasonable interpretation of an unlegislated
   announcement; the actual rules may differ.
3. **30% minimum interpreted as `max(MTR, 0.30)`.** "30% minimum tax on net
   capital gains" is ambiguous. Alternative reading: 30% flat on the indexed
   gain regardless of MTR. The chosen interpretation is the standard reading
   (a floor, not a flat rate) and treats high-MTR earners normally.
4. **Quarantined losses offset indexed gain *before* the rate is applied.**
   Reduces the gain dollar-for-dollar at the high (37%+) rate rather than at
   the discount-equivalent rate. Best-faith reading; defensible.
5. **Year 1 grace for a today-purchase.** Property bought May 2026 has FY2027
   under current rules (1 year of full NG benefit) before restriction kicks in
   1 Jul 2027 / FY2028. Configurable via parameter.
6. **No CGT changes for grandfathered or new-build properties.** They keep the
   50% discount under the carve-out. Same toggle covers both: pick "Current
   rules" if buying new build or pre-cutoff.

---

## Out of scope (explicit)

- **CPI assumed constant** at the user's input rate over the full horizon.
  Stochastic CPI not modelled.
- **Build-to-rent / super / widely-held trust carve-outs** — out of scope for a
  personal-use tool.
- **Pre-existing v1 backlog items** (stamp duty in CGT cost base, dual-seed
  RNG, `rental_yield_sigma` slider no-op): orthogonal — handled separately.
- **CGT changes for pre-cutoff acquisitions** — fully grandfathered per the
  announcement; no change required.
- **Other state taxes** (still SA-only).

---

## Caveats surfaced in code, UI, and README

- The new regime is the **default** in the UI (matches the realistic case for
  a today-purchase of an established property).
- Both NG and CGT changes are **announcement-only as of 12 May 2026**. The
  banner, README, and code comments flag this. The model needs revisiting if
  the legislation is amended.
